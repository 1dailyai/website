A dual nature

A commodity that works like money.

Gold has a unique dual nature. On the one hand, it is a physical commodity whose price is set by global supply and demand. On the other, it is a monetary safe haven that protects against inflation, currency debasement and geopolitical crises.

01

Commodity

In 2025, mines worldwide produced 3,672 tonnes of gold, more than ever before. Around 1,400 tonnes more came from recycling. Gold is in demand for jewellery, bars and coins, technology and as a central bank reserve.

02

Safe haven

Central banks hold around 39,000 tonnes of gold, 18% of all gold above ground. In 2025 they bought another 863 tonnes. In crises and when inflation rises, capital has traditionally flowed into gold.

03

Currency

XAU is the official ISO currency code for one troy ounce of gold, or 31.1035 grams. That is why gold is quoted like a currency pair: XAUUSD shows how many US dollars one ounce costs.

Trading venues worldwide

Where gold is traded. All around the globe.

Gold trades over the counter between banks, on futures exchanges and through physically backed products. Together, these venues form the global market price.

  • London

    LBMA · London Bullion Market
    OTCBenchmark

    The world’s largest over-the-counter gold market. The LBMA Gold Price is set twice a day, at 10:30 and 15:00 London time, and serves as the global benchmark. London vaults hold around 9,464 tonnes of gold.

  • New York

    COMEX · CME Group
    Futures

    The most important futures exchange for gold. A standard contract covers 100 troy ounces. During US trading hours, much of the price discovery happens here.

  • Shanghai

    SGE · Shanghai Futures Exchange
    PhysicalFutures

    The Shanghai Gold Exchange is the largest exchange for physical gold, while the Shanghai Futures Exchange trades gold futures in yuan. China is the world’s largest gold producer.

  • Switzerland

    Zurich · Ticino · Neuchâtel
    RefiningTrading

    Four of the world’s largest gold refineries are based in Switzerland. Depending on the estimate, 40 to 70 percent of the gold traded worldwide is processed here.

  • Frankfurt

    Deutsche Börse · Xetra-Gold
    SecurityPhysical

    Xetra-Gold is an exchange-traded security fully backed by physical gold. Germany is one of Europe’s largest markets for gold bars and coins.

  • Dubai

    DGCX · DMCC
    FuturesPhysical

    Dubai is the hub of the gold trade between Africa, Asia and Europe. Gold futures are traded on the Dubai Gold and Commodities Exchange.

  • Mumbai

    MCX · Multi Commodity Exchange
    Futures

    The Multi Commodity Exchange is India’s largest commodity exchange. Together with China, India is one of the largest gold markets in the world.

  • Osaka

    JPX · Osaka Exchange
    Futures

    Since 2020, Japanese gold futures have traded on the Osaka Exchange of the Japan Exchange Group. Japan shapes early Asian trading.

  • Istanbul

    Borsa İstanbul
    Physical

    The precious metals market of Borsa İstanbul connects Europe and Asia. Turkey is one of the largest buyers of gold jewellery, bars and coins.

Trading hours

A market that hardly sleeps.

XAUUSD trades almost around the clock from Monday to Friday, with a short daily break. Liquidity follows the sun from Asia to Europe to the Americas. The strongest moves usually occur when London and New York trade at the same time.

Price drivers

What moves the gold price.

01
Interest rates and central banks

Gold pays no interest. When real interest rates rise, it becomes relatively less attractive. Decisions and statements by the US Federal Reserve often move the price within minutes.

02
US dollar

Gold is quoted in US dollars. A weaker dollar makes gold cheaper for buyers in other currencies and supports the price. A stronger dollar has the opposite effect.

03
Economic data

US labour market data, inflation figures and growth data regularly trigger the strongest moves of the day.

04
Inflation and purchasing power

Gold has served as a store of value for thousands of years. Rising inflation and concerns about the purchasing power of paper currencies increase demand.

05
Geopolitics

Wars, trade disputes and political crises steer capital into the safe haven. Such moves often begin abruptly.

06
Central banks and ETFs

Central bank purchases and flows into and out of gold ETFs shift supply and demand noticeably, often over months.

XAUUSD in detail

How CFD trading on gold works.

01

Contract for difference

A CFD tracks the price movement of gold without delivery of physical gold. What is settled is the difference between the entry and exit price.

02

Long and short

Positions are possible in both directions: on rising prices (long) and on falling prices (short). That is why XAUUSD is particularly well suited to day trading.

03

Leverage and margin

Only part of a position’s value is deposited as collateral. Leverage magnifies gains and losses to the same degree.

04

Contract size

At most providers, one standard lot equals 100 troy ounces. If the gold price moves by USD 1, the value of such a position changes by USD 100.
